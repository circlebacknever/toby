# Comedy and narrative

In-game text (cards, news tickers, upgrade labels, and end screens) uses the wider comedy register: dry, specific satire aimed at broken systems and bureaucratic procedures, and never at the player. Every line must reflect a real state change or mechanic in the game.

## Reversals and targets

- **The reversal:** state an official rule in a flat bureaucratic tone, then contradict it in a shorter second clause (`"The appeals committee is Gerald. The appeal is granted."`).
- **Specific procedures:** aim at a concrete mechanism (such as a prior-authorization fax desk or form `27B/6`) and borrow its real euphemisms (`"for quality and training purposes"`). Write the low-level clerk as someone trapped inside the same rules.

## Storylines and tickers

- **Escalating story threads:** advance each storyline one milestone at a time (a pothole is logged, assigned a caseworker, then subpoenaed), and bring an early detail back at the finale. Draw flavor lines without replacement so jokes never repeat, and give story milestones priority over flavor lines.
- **Event-driven ticker:** fire a wire-service ticker line (`NEWS:`, `311:`, `MEMO:`) as soon as a game threshold trips or a number shifts. Rate-limit rapid events, and allow at most one ambient line after 30 seconds of inactivity.
- **Character blurbs and graded endings:** define each character with a few tuned floats and one specific line triggered when their trait affects play. Track three to five player habits across the run and award a tailored rank title on the end screen based on those stats.

## The dread register

In the dread register, keep the bureaucratic reversal structure while removing the punchline (`"The building is fully evacuated. Occupancy: 1,204. Last updated three minutes ago."`). When the narrative turns, stop idle motion and ambient audio, let unlabeled red markers appear where the player didn't act, and end with an ungraded verdict.

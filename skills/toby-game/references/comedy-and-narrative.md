# Comedy and narrative — the voice

House rules govern how Toby talks to you while building. The game's own copy is a separate register you set, funnier and warmer and louder. Generated work keeps Toby's voice unless you ask for another, and a game is you asking. Cards, ticker, upgrade names, and the end screen get the wider voice. Two rules hold under it. Punch up at the system, never down at the person. The joke and the mechanic are one thing. A line that names what a number just did stays. A line you could delete without losing what happened goes.

**The reversal.** State an official fact flat, then let the second clause detonate it. The structure carries the joke, so the prose never winks.

- "the appeals committee is Gerald. the appeal is granted."
- "311: thank you for reporting the raccoon. the raccoon has also reported you."
- "Per policy, dying is covered. Staying alive required prior authorization."

The turn comes last, the second clause runs shorter, and every word the joke survives without is cut.

**Find the real thing to aim at.** "Insurance" is a fog bank. The prior-authorization desk is a target. Name the specific mechanism, then steal its real texture — actual form numbers, the euphemisms it hides behind ("not medically necessary," "for quality and training purposes"), the fax-only window that opens after it closes. The 27B/6 joke works because 27B/6 sounds like a real form. Generic codes go toothless. Aim at the desk and the policy, and let the clerk reading the script sound trapped too.

**A run is a story.** Serialize the threads. A storyline advances one step at a time, on milestones, fired once. Escalate: each beat out-scales the last (the pothole gets logged, then a caseworker, then standing in court), and a beat that does not climb gets cut. Plant a throwaway early and pay it off transformed at the finale. Draw flavor without replacement so nothing recurs. Give plot beats a priority lane that jumps the flavor queue, or a growing feed starves the ending.

**Narrate sparingly.** A dry wire-service ticker (NEWS:, 311:, MEMO:) reports the world neutral in tone and quietly on the underdog's side. The gap is the butt, never the person. Fire it on the beat, and gate every line on a real change — a number moved, a threshold tripped, a thread advanced — so an idle moment stays silent. Pace it: an ambient wire line every twenty-five to forty seconds, an event line the instant its trigger fires and then rate-limited, the gaps between left alone. A line a second is noise. A line every generation when nothing changed is the feed flashing. Even a silent toy carries the voice in its labels.

**Characters are a number and a line.** Personality is a few tuned floats and a blurb that names what the float does ("his aim drifts on its own. so does Randy."). Give each one tender, exact line, fired when their flaw bites. Without it the deadpan reads cold.

**The ending reads the run back.** Track three to five things during play — what they optimized, what they sacrificed, their tell. Close on a graded rank laned by how they played, with a verdict in their own stats. A PROBATIONARY BLEEDING HEART and a FUNCTIONING OPERATOR are different people. Land warm unless the premise earns bleak. Heavier real-world weight pulls the tone flatter.

**The dread register.** At the dark end the machine inverts. The reversal stops detonating and starts dawning. The second clause arrives a beat too late and is not funny ("The building is fully evacuated. Occupancy: 1,204. Last updated three minutes ago."). The ticker knows things it should not. The advance has no ceiling. At the turn everything goes still and silent and the reward never comes. The ending convicts — a verdict, sitting outside the graded-rank scale used elsewhere.

# Comedy and narrative — the voice

The text on cards, the news ticker, upgrade names, and the end screen uses the wider register. The wider register is a looser, joking style than Toby's chat replies.

In the wider register, make jokes about the system and never about the person. Tie every joke to a mechanic. Keep a line that reports how a game value just changed, and cut a line you could delete without losing what happened.

**The reversal.** State an official fact in a neutral tone, then make the second half of the sentence contradict it. The structure makes the joke, so the prose never signals that it is joking.

- "the appeals committee is Gerald. the appeal is granted."
- "311: thank you for reporting the raccoon. the raccoon has also reported you."
- "Per policy, dying is covered. Staying alive required prior authorization."

The surprising part comes last, the second half is shorter, and every word the joke does not need is cut.

**Find the real thing to aim at.** "Insurance" is too vague to aim at, but the prior-authorization desk is a target. Pick the mechanism, then copy its real details, such as actual form numbers, the euphemisms it uses ("not medically necessary," "for quality and training purposes"), and the fax-only window that opens after it closes. A form number such as "27B/6" is funny because it sounds like a real form. Generic codes are not funny. Aim at the desk and the policy, and write the clerk who follows the official script as someone trapped in the system too.

**Build the run as a story.** Run each storyline as a sequence of steps. A storyline advances one step at a time, with each step firing once at a milestone. Escalate, so each step is bigger than the last (the pothole gets logged, then assigned a caseworker, then taken to court), and cut a step that does not escalate. Introduce a throwaway detail early and bring it back, transformed, at the finale. Pick each flavor line at random from the lines not yet shown, so no line appears twice. Give story steps priority over flavor lines in the queue, or a growing feed leaves no room for the ending.

**Narrate sparingly.** A dry wire-service ticker (NEWS:, 311:, MEMO:) reports on the world in a neutral tone while quietly siding with the underdog. The joke comes from the gap between the neutral tone and the ticker's hidden sympathy for the underdog, and the joke never mocks the player. Fire the ticker at the moment something happens, and gate every line on a real change, such as a moved number, a tripped threshold, or an advanced thread. An idle moment then stays silent. Pace the ticker with one background news line every twenty-five to forty seconds. Show an event line the instant its trigger fires, rate-limit event lines after that, and leave the gaps between them empty. A silent toy still uses the voice in its labels.

**Build each character from a few numbers and one line.** Personality is a few tuned floats and a blurb that says what the float does ("his aim drifts on its own. so does Randy."). Give each one a tender, exact line, fired when their flaw affects play. Without that line, the deadpan comes across as cold.

**The ending reports on the run.** Track three to five things during play, such as what they optimized, what they sacrificed, and the habit that shows how they play. End with a graded rank title, chosen from a separate list of ranks for each way of playing. State the verdict in the player's own stats. A PROBATIONARY BLEEDING HEART and a FUNCTIONING OPERATOR describe different ways of playing. End warm unless the premise calls for a bleak ending. When the premise has heavier real-world weight, make the tone flatter.

**The dread register.** The dread register is the darkest tone. In this tone, the jokes keep their structure and stop being funny. The reversal no longer surprises, and its meaning becomes clear slowly. The second half arrives a moment too late and is not funny ("The building is fully evacuated. Occupancy: 1,204. Last updated three minutes ago."). The ticker reports things it should not know. The advance never reaches a limit. At the moment the story turns, all motion and sound stop. The reward never comes. The ending delivers a guilty verdict outside the graded-rank scale used elsewhere.

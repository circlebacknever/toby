# Cross-device — one live game, two screens

A game that never pauses cannot put its controls in a menu. The layout and the controls differ between a wide screen and a phone while the game stays live on both.

**Read-state can collapse, but live controls stay on screen.** Scores, rates, and the catalog you browse between actions can fold into a sheet. Any verb the player uses during play stays on screen and moves to where the hand is. A control that the player has to stop and open with a gesture only works in a paused game, but this game is not paused. Panels can overlap on screen, but only one on-screen surface receives input for the live verb at a time, so a tap never registers in two places.

**Keep what the player needs out from under a busy thumb.** On a phone, the live controls go in the two bottom corners, clear of the home indicator. The must-see action renders in the band above them. A thumb holding a stick covers the pixels under it, so nothing critical goes there.

**Lay out per screen.** On a portrait phone, put controls in the bottom corners and lift the camera so the action shows in the clear band. On a phone held sideways, drop the bottom sheet and put controls in the side gutters. Pull the camera back so the play still fits in the short dimension. On a wide desktop, show the scene with mouse and keyboard, and put read-state beside it. Clamp an ultrawide so the playfield does not stretch. As the view narrows, widen the field of view or pull the camera back so a five-inch screen stays legible. Size gameplay-critical things to be seen and hit at arm's length. Measure the chrome. Read the non-transitioning panels into a CSS variable and re-measure on resize, or a hardcoded dock height puts a button on top of the controls. Clear the home indicator with `env(safe-area-inset-*)`, and cap a portrait bottom sheet near 40–46vh so it never covers the play. Set minimum sizes for the type and the hit targets for a thumb (roughly a 12px body, 15px panel titles, 44px targets). Before shipping, check three real sizes, which are 390×844 portrait, 768×1024 tablet, and 1280×800 desktop. Look for overlapped controls, cropped panels, and buttons the thumb cannot reach.

**Each archetype needs its own layout.** A twitch game puts live controls under both thumbs with the scene clear between them. A manager sim pins the speed knob and the buy verbs and moves the browsing into tabs. A sandbox keeps its sliders thumb-reachable while it runs. Choose the controls from the player relationship, and choose where they go from the screen.

**Performance is part of the layout.** Cap the pixel ratio, scale particle and shadow budgets down on a phone, and cut detail before frame rate when performance is tight. A phone at 20fps is unplayable however good the layout looks. When pinch-zoom is off, give a live game its own way to see a board bigger than the screen.

Test the game live on a phone in both orientations, with a player using touch. A game tuned on a mouse can be unwinnable on a thumb.
